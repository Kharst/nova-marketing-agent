How to Build a Solar Proposal That Gets a Same‑Week Yes

By Nova Metrics19 October 20263 min read

A well‑structured proposal is the fastest path to a client’s commitment. Installers who present the right information in the right order can move a prospect from interest to signed agreement within a single week. This article breaks down the essential parts of a South African solar proposal and shows how to communicate them efficiently.

Start With the Numbers That Matter

Homeowners focus on the monthly saving and the year when the system pays for itself. Place those figures front and centre on the cover page. For businesses, the financial decision hinges on the projected net present value, internal rate of return and the tax benefit from Section 12B. By leading with the metric each audience cares about, you give them a clear reason to say yes quickly.

  • Residential cover: system size, monthly saving, backup hours, payback year
  • Commercial cover: executive summary, 25‑year ROI chart, NPV, IRR, Section 12B impact

Show That the Design Passes Required Checks

Installers need to demonstrate that the system meets the technical standards that regulators reference. Solar Intelligence flags any issue with the DC/AC ratio, battery depth of discharge or other key parameters, and displays a pass/fail result on the proposal. Including that visual confirmation removes the need for a separate compliance discussion during the meeting.

Explain the Backup Validation Clearly

If the client has raised concerns about power outages, point them to the backup validation section. The proposal shows a pass/fail status for battery backup based on the required runtime and safety criteria. This single line lets the client see that the design will keep essential loads running when the grid is down.

Provide Full Assumptions and Sources

Transparency builds trust. The proposal lists every assumption—such as the specific yield, performance ratio and availability—next to the source it comes from, whether that is the SAURAN Solar Atlas or a national tariff schedule. When the numbers are sourced, the client can verify them without you having to repeat the calculations.

  • Specific yield, performance ratio, availability
  • Tariff escalation assumptions
  • Section 12B tax treatment

End With a Concrete Next Step

The final page of the proposal outlines a five‑step install process, required municipal paperwork and a signature block. Close the meeting by confirming a date for the client to sign and schedule the site visit. Securing that calendar slot before the discussion ends turns interest into action.

Common questions

What key figures should be highlighted on a residential solar proposal?

Show the monthly saving and the estimated payback year on the cover page, as those are the numbers homeowners remember most.

How can an installer demonstrate that a design meets technical standards?

Include the pass/fail results from the system checks, such as the DC/AC ratio and battery backup validation, directly on the proposal.

What information helps a commercial client decide quickly?

Present the net present value, internal rate of return and the effective capital cost after Section 12B tax relief alongside the equipment cost.
