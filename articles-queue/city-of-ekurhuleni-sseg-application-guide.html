City of Ekurhuleni SSEG application

By Nova Metrics2 November 20263 min read

Preparing a City of Ekurhuleni SSEG application involves several specific documents and post‑installation checks. Missing a single form can delay registration and push back your project timeline. This guide walks you through each required item, from the initial online submission to the final commissioning pack, so you can keep your paperwork in order and focus on installing clean energy.

1. Submit the application online

All SSEG systems below 1 MVA are registered through the online portal at apply.sseg.org.za. The portal captures basic project details and initiates the review process. Ensure the information entered matches the design you intend to install, as any discrepancy will be flagged during the technical check stage.

  • Use apply.sseg.org.za for all applications under 1 MVA
  • Enter project details that reflect the final design

2. Annexure 4 – Notice of Commencement of Installation Work

Annexure 4 is a mandatory form that confirms who will carry out the electrical work. The electrician responsible for the installation must complete and sign this document. It is then attached to the online application before the City can issue a Permission to Install letter.

  • Completed and signed by the installing electrician
  • Attached to the online application before approval

3. Equipment certification and inverter testing

The City requires a type‑test certificate for every inverter, confirming it meets NRS 097‑2‑1 standards. The certificate must be issued by an accredited third‑party test house. If your system includes a storage inverter that runs in parallel with the PV inverter, a separate NRS 097‑2‑1 certificate is also needed for that unit.

  • Inverter type‑test certificate (NRS 097‑2‑1)
  • Separate certificate for storage inverter if applicable

4. As‑built circuit diagram and safety labels

A detailed as‑built diagram is required to show the point of connection to the municipal network, all protection devices, breakers, isolators, disconnectors, measurement locations and the SSEG connection point within the total system. After installation, the City asks for safety labels at the municipal supply point. These labels must display the dual‑supply indication and be positioned in line with the NRS 097‑2‑1 requirements for safety labeling.

  • Diagram must include connection point, protection devices and measurement locations
  • Safety labels showing dual supply at the municipal supply point

5. Export or non‑export meter and commissioning pack

If the system is intended to export electricity, the City installs a four‑quadrant bi‑directional AMR meter at the customer's cost. For non‑export installations, reverse power flow blocking protection must be fitted. After the work is completed, the installer must submit a signed commissioning form, the Certificate of Compliance (CoC) for the PV installation, photographs of the safety labels, and the final as‑built diagram. The commissioning declaration also includes an on‑site anti‑islanding test and a momentary mains disconnection to verify safe operation.

  • Exporting systems need a bi‑directional AMR meter installed by the City
  • Non‑export systems require reverse power flow blocking protection
  • Submit commissioning form, CoC, label photos and final diagram

Common questions

What form must the installing electrician sign for a City of Ekurhuleni SSEG application?

The electrician must complete and sign Annexure 4, the Notice of Commencement of Installation Work, and attach it to the online application.

Which certificates are required for inverters in an Ekurhuleni SSEG project?

A type‑test certificate for each inverter that meets NRS 097‑2‑1, issued by an accredited test house; a separate certificate is needed for a storage inverter that runs in parallel.

What meter is required for an exporting SSEG system in Ekurhuleni?

The City installs a four‑quadrant bi‑directional AMR meter at the customer's cost for exporting systems.
