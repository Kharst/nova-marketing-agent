Accurate PV and Battery Sizing for South African Installers

By Nova Metrics · 10 October 2026 · 3 min read

Sizing a solar PV and battery system can feel like solving a puzzle with many moving parts. Getting the panel count, inverter rating and battery capacity right at the quote stage saves time, reduces re‑work and builds confidence with clients. This guide walks you through the key checks an installer should perform, and shows where automation can help keep the process transparent.

Start with the panel array

The first number you need is the total DC array size. It is simply the number of panels multiplied by the panel wattage, expressed in kilowatts. This figure feeds every downstream calculation – from expected production to the financial model that the client sees. Because it is the foundation, double‑checking the panel model and count before you move on is essential.”

Set the right DC/AC ratio

A reliable rule of thumb is to target a DC‑to‑AC ratio of about 1.3 : 1. That means the DC array is roughly 30 % larger than the inverter’s rated AC output. In practice, this oversizing captures more energy during mornings, evenings and cloudy periods while limiting the amount of clipping on the sunniest days.

The guide flags a ratio outside the range of 1.0 – 1.5 for review. If your proposal lands below 1.0, the inverter may be oversized relative to the panels. If it lands above 1.5, you likely need fewer panels or a larger inverter. Either way, the ratio is a sizing question, not a formality – a second look at panel count or inverter selection is required before quoting.

Validate battery headroom and backup hours

Battery sizing should reflect the client’s actual outage needs, not just the battery’s nominal capacity. Calculate the evening energy required by multiplying the peak load (in kilowatts) by the requested backup hours and a load factor of roughly 0.7. This factor accounts for the fact that not every appliance runs at full draw simultaneously.

Next, compute the headroom ratio by dividing the installed battery capacity by the evening energy required. A ratio of 1.0 or higher indicates the battery can meet the client’s stated autonomy. The platform checks that the depth of discharge stays within the 85 % limit for LiFePO4 chemistry and flags any design that falls short of 98 % of the requested runtime. If the runtime is lower, the proposal shows a ‘Review sizing’ notice instead of a silent pass.

Consider future loads such as electric vehicle chargers or pool pumps. Adding a modest amount of extra capacity now avoids a difficult conversation later when the client wants to expand the system.

Confirm MPPT and string configuration

While the overall DC/AC ratio is automatically checked, string layout remains an installer responsibility. Match panel voltage and current to the inverter’s MPPT input window, using the specific panel and inverter datasheets. This step ensures the inverter operates efficiently and avoids clipping at the string level.

Use the proposal as a quick sanity check

Every generated proposal displays the key checks: the DC/AC ratio, the battery backup validation bar and a pass/fail status. When in doubt, open the proposal – it will not silently approve a design that falls outside the recommended ranges. This visual cue gives you confidence that the system is sized correctly before you move to site visits and installation scheduling.

Common questions

What DC/AC ratio should be used for South African residential systems?

Aim for a ratio around 1.3 : 1, and keep the final value between 1.0 and 1.5; values outside this range should be reviewed.

How is battery headroom calculated for backup autonomy?

Divide the installed battery capacity by the evening energy required (peak load × backup hours × 0.7); a ratio of 1.0 or higher is acceptable.

What does a proposal show when battery runtime is insufficient?

The proposal displays a ‘Review sizing’ notice instead of a pass, indicating the design needs adjustment.
