Accurate PV and Battery Sizing for South African Installers

By Nova Metrics · 10 October 2026 · 3 min read

Sizing a solar system correctly is the foundation of a reliable installation. Small mistakes in panel count or battery capacity can lead to under‑performance, client disappointment and costly re‑work. This article walks you through the calculations and checks you need to perform before you submit a quote, so the design complies with NRS 097-2-1 and meets the client’s backup expectations.

Start with the Panel Array

The first number you need is the DC array size. It is simply the number of panels multiplied by the panel’s wattage, then converted to kilowatts. This figure drives every downstream calculation – from projected production to financial return. Getting the panel model and count right at the quote stage matters more than any other single input.

  • Panel count × panel wattage = DC array size (kW)
  • Use the exact module specification the client wants

Apply the Correct DC/AC Ratio

In South Africa the recommended DC‑to‑AC ratio is about 1.3:1. That means the DC array should be roughly 30 % larger than the inverter’s rated AC output. Oversizing captures more energy during low‑light periods while only a small amount is clipped on the brightest days. The acceptable range is 1.0 to 1.5, as defined by NRS 097-2-1:2017. If your ratio falls outside this window, revisit the panel count or inverter rating before quoting.

  • Target ratio ≈ 1.3:1
  • Compliant range 1.0 – 1.5
  • Re‑check if outside range

Determine Battery Headroom and Backup Hours

Battery sizing must reflect the client’s actual outage needs, not just the battery’s nominal capacity. First calculate the evening energy required: multiply the peak load (kW) by the requested backup hours and then by a load factor of roughly 0.7. This factor accounts for the fact that not all loads run at full power simultaneously. Next, compute the headroom ratio by dividing the installed battery capacity by the evening energy required. A ratio of 1.0 or higher is considered safe.

The platform checks that the depth of discharge does not exceed 85 % for LiFePO4 chemistry and that the projected runtime reaches at least 98 % of the client’s requested autonomy. If either check fails, the proposal flags the sizing for review instead of passing silently.

  • Evening energy = peak load × backup hours × 0.7
  • Headroom ratio = installed capacity ÷ evening energy
  • Target headroom ≥ 1.0
  • DoD limit 85 % for LiFePO4
  • Runtime target ≥ 98 % of requested autonomy

Validate MPPT Compatibility and Final Checks

While the software confirms the overall DC/AC ratio and capacity, the string layout remains an installer responsibility. Match panel string voltage and current to the inverter’s MPPT input window using the inverter manufacturer’s datasheet. Once the design is complete, the generated proposal displays the DC/AC ratio, battery backup validation bar and a pass/fail status. Reviewing these indicators is the quickest way to confirm that the system complies with standards before you hand the quote to the client.

  • Confirm string voltage/current against MPPT window
  • Review proposal’s pass/fail status for DC/AC and battery checks

Common questions

What DC/AC ratio should be used for South African solar proposals?

Aim for a ratio of about 1.3:1, staying within the compliant range of 1.0 to 1.5 as defined by NRS 097-2-1.

How is battery headroom calculated for backup sizing?

Divide the installed battery capacity by the evening energy requirement (peak load × backup hours × 0.7); a ratio of 1.0 or higher provides sufficient headroom.

What runtime validation does the platform enforce for battery backup?

It requires the projected runtime to meet at least 98 % of the client’s requested autonomy and limits depth of discharge to 85 % for LiFePO4 batteries.
