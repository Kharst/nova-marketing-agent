City of Cape Town SSEG application

By Nova Metrics31 October 20263 min read

The City of Cape Town has tightened its rules for small‑scale embedded generation (SSEG). For installers, understanding each requirement prevents delays and protects the week’s work. This guide walks you through the complete process – from the first check on system type to the final commissioning approval letter – using the checks built into Solar Intelligence where relevant.

1. Confirm what the City still accepts

Since 1 October 2023 the City treats every PV system on a supply point as grid‑tied. Off‑grid and standby PV installations are no longer accepted. The only off‑grid exception is a solar PV geyser. The first step is to verify that the proposed design fits this grid‑tied model before any paperwork is started.

  • Off‑grid PV → not accepted (except solar geysers)
  • All systems must connect to the municipal grid

2. Secure the correct signatures and submit the application

The SSEG application must be signed by the property owner. If you act on the owner’s behalf, attach an approved letter of proxy. Applications are filed on the City’s online SSEG platform. Submitting without the owner’s signature or a valid proxy will cause the application to be rejected.

  • Owner’s signature required
  • Letter of proxy needed when installer applies

3. Choose the right inverter and decide on export

Every inverter used in Cape Town must be type‑tested to NRS 097‑2‑1 by an accredited body and appear on the City’s approved inverter list, which is refreshed each month. Check the list early to avoid last‑minute substitutions. Next, decide whether the system will export electricity. Exporting systems need a bi‑directional AMI meter installed at the customer’s cost. Non‑export systems must include reverse‑power‑flow blocking protection.

  • Inverter must be on the monthly approved list
  • Exporting → bi‑directional AMI meter (customer pays)
  • Non‑export → reverse power flow protection

4. Obtain the Permission to Install letter

After the online application is accepted, the City issues a Permission to Install letter. Do not purchase equipment before this letter arrives; approval is not assured until it is issued. Once you have the letter, you have three months to complete registration, otherwise the application is cancelled.

  • Wait for the Permission to Install letter before buying hardware
  • Complete registration within three months

5. Complete commissioning and collect the approval letter

In Cape Town, a grid‑tied system must remain disconnected after installation until a Commissioning Approval Letter is issued. Generating power before the letter results in a contravention notice and a service fee. The City requires an ECSA‑registered professional sign‑off – an engineer, engineering technologist or certified engineer for all system sizes, or a professional technician for residential installations only. After successful on‑site testing and verification, the City provides the final approval letter, allowing the system to be energized.

  • System stays offline until approval letter is received
  • ECSA professional sign‑off required

Common questions

What signature is required on a City of Cape Town SSEG application?

The property owner must sign the application, or the installer must attach an approved letter of proxy if applying on the owner’s behalf.

Which inverter certification does the City of Cape Town require?

Inverters must be type‑tested to NRS 097‑2‑1 by an accredited body and listed on the City’s approved inverter list.

When can a newly installed system be connected to the grid?

Only after the City issues the Commissioning Approval Letter following the required ECSA professional sign‑off and on‑site testing.
